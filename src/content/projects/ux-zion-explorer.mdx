## The idea

The useful thing an app can tell you about Angels Landing is not what it looks
like. It is that you need a lottery permit, that it gains 1,500 feet, and that
the chains section is the part people turn around on.

So the trail overview leads with distance, difficulty, elevation gain, duration
and permit status — the five facts that decide whether the hike is happening —
and the photograph supports them rather than replacing them. The Narrows gets
the same treatment for a different reason: it is a walk up a river, it closes on
cubic-feet-per-second, and that number is the whole planning decision.

The information screen exists because national parks publish rules that matter
— shuttle timetables, weather and flash-flood warnings, visitor services — and
most trail apps make you leave for the park's own site to find them.
